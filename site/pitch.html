01  /  The problem has teeth

Missing the date is not an inconvenience. It is a cause of action.

Miss the sixty days and the district owes compensatory services, faces a due process complaint, and lands on a state corrective action plan. Roughly twenty states override the federal count with their own — 45 school days, 30 business days — and school days pause across every break on that district's calendar.

Washington moved to 35 school days in June 2026. Every one of those is different arithmetic on the same form.

The clock runs from the day the district receives consent, not the day the parent signed.

That is 34 CFR §300.301(c)(1)(i). We had it wrong for eight days, and found it by building the intake path and watching a legible form get refused. Every case page now says which date started the clock and why.

02  /  Why nobody has done it

They need an autonomous agent more than almost anyone. They will never be given one.

The records are minors' clinical files. The buyer has no security staff, no budget line for AI, and a legal obligation that makes a wrong answer expensive. Every vendor that could serve them is selling to someone easier.

So the hard part was never the agents. It was the guardrails that make pointing them at real student records something a district counsel will sign.

03  /  The business argument

Compliance is a cost centre. Cost centres get cut.

Districts can bill Medicaid for the very services these deadlines govern, and most underclaim badly. A single New York City audit found $431.6 million unclaimed — and $1.2 billion since 2011.

The reimbursement requirements are, almost word for word, what a compliance system already holds: the service is in the IEP, it was delivered as written, it was documented. Same child, same records, second question.

Compliance stops the lawsuits. Claiming is what pays for us.

That pairing is the product. It turns the pitch from "please find budget" into "we stop the lawsuits and we find the money that funds us." Cost settlement compounds it: underclaiming one year lowers the ceiling for the next.

04  /  The wedge nobody else is selling

The risk is not only leaving money on the table. It is taking money you cannot defend.

Federal audits flagged $1.5 billion in improper or unsupported school-based Medicaid claims across Pennsylvania, New York, New Jersey and Michigan.

Every competitor sells maximisation. We gate. Each delivered session is checked against eligibility, NPI, licence validity on the service date, provider type, the IEP window, and units against documented minutes — then a model reads the note and asks whether it describes the service the IEP authorised.

One session passes every rule check. Eligible student. Licensed provider. Correct units. A properly written note. It is still a denial: the note describes a GROUP session, the IEP authorises INDIVIDUAL. No pattern matches a story.

Over-billing blocks. Under-billing is surfaced as revenue left behind. Nothing is submitted — the export is what a billing vendor ingests, and export is not submission. The district keeps that decision and the liability with it.

06  /  The moat

Authorisation is not enough. The data is shaped to the identity.

The family-facing agent does not receive clinical fields and decline to use them. It never receives them.

A check can be forgotten at a new call site; a projection cannot leak a field it never returned. Field classification fails closed, so a field nobody has classified yet is withheld rather than exposed — a default that caught a real gap the first table missed.

A parent may read their own child and no other. That returns 404, not 403, because 403 would confirm the other child exists.

08  /  What is not built

Stated plainly, because several read as finished from the architecture alone.

01

No multi-tenancy

Every collection is global. One district is fine; two is a breach. This is the first thing built next, and it gates everything.

02

Medicaid billing consent is not modelled

IDEA §300.154 requires a consent to bill that is separate from consent to evaluate. The readiness gate does not check it yet, which means it would pass a session a real audit would recoup.

03

Three of four state rules are invented

Only the federal sixty-day baseline is accurate. The counting engine handles calendar days, school days, business days and break pauses — the rules themselves need legal review, per state.

04

No SOC 2, no signed DPA, no SIS integration

78% of district technology officers require SOC 2 Type II, rising to 94% above ten thousand students. That is nine to fourteen months and $30–80k, and no amount of engineering shortens it.

05

Agent Identity and Agent Gateway are substituted

Agent identity is registry-declared, not attested — what the gateway enforces is a scope table, not zero-trust. The managed APIs are not offered on a personal Cloud account.

Everything above is checkable. scripts/geap.sh fetches the platform evidence at run time and prints the two components we lack just as plainly as the four we have.
